## Tokens Are the Bill

Module 08 taught the meter: you pay per token, in AND out. A one-off chat doesn't care. A production prompt (lesson 1) running 100,000 times a day multiplies every token by 100,000.

800 wasted tokens becomes 80 million wasted tokens daily. At scale, leanness is an engineering requirement, not a style preference.

## Where Bloat Hides

Look for:

- **Apologetic filler** — "Please, if you don't mind..." adds nothing but cost
- **The same rule stated three ways** — say it once, precisely
- **Examples that demonstrate nothing new** — if example 3 shows the same pattern as examples 1 and 2, it's waste
- **Boilerplate from a 'great prompts' listicle** — pasted advice someone else's problem, not yours
- **Instructions for situations that can't occur** — "If the input is a JPEG image..." in a prompt that only ever receives text

Each survived because nobody was paying per copy. At production scale, every unnecessary word is visible in the bill.

## Caching Intuition

Providers can CACHE the processed form of a prompt's beginning. If today's request starts with the same tokens as the last one, that prefix is nearly free instead of full price.

The design rule falls out immediately: **FIXED CONTENT FIRST, VARIABLES LAST**.

A timestamp or user name at the TOP of the prompt breaks the match at token ten and the whole prefix reprocesses at full price.

```
❌ BAD (cache breaks immediately):
Request at: 2024-07-31 14:22:03
User: dana@example.com

You are a support triage system. [rules...]
Ticket: {{ticket_text}}

✅ GOOD (stable prefix caches):
You are a support triage system. [rules...]

User: dana@example.com
Ticket: {{ticket_text}}
```

Module 10's reason to be lean was attention budget. The accountant's reason to be lean is cache alignment.

<Callout kind="tip">
Caching benefits compound over thousands of calls. A well-ordered prompt with a 400-token stable prefix turns 400 full-price tokens into 400 cache-hit tokens per call. At 100,000 calls, that's 40 million tokens hitting cache instead of reprocessing at full rate.
</Callout>

## Leanness ≠ Omission

Cutting a requirement isn't optimization, it's breakage. The eval suite (lesson 4) is what proves your lean rewrite still passes.

Say once, precisely, what you used to say three times, vaguely. Remove filler. Remove redundancy. Keep every requirement.
