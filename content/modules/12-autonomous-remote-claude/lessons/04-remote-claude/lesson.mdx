## Tier 1: local CLI

Everything so far: `claude` in your terminal, working on YOUR files, with your permission settings. Full power, zero distance — you must be at the machine.

## Tier 2: Claude Code on the web

Sessions run on Anthropic's cloud infrastructure against a FRESH CLONE of your connected repo — not your working tree. Start a task from a browser tab, close the laptop, come back to a proposed change. The fresh-clone detail matters: uncommitted local changes don't exist there; work lands as branches/PRs, not edits to your disk.

This is what makes machine-off execution possible — and why you can't ask the web tier to "fix that typo I just made but haven't committed."

## Tier 3: Remote Control

`/remote-control` in a LOCAL session generates a pairing QR code / link; from a browser or your phone you attach to that very session — same working tree, same context, your machine does the work while you steer from the sofa or the train. Distance for your hands, not for the session.

The session stays local; only your input and the display travel over the wire.

## The connective tissue: `--resume`

Sessions are addressable and portable (module 09 taught local resume). In current versions you can resume across surfaces — pick up in the CLI a session you started elsewhere. The session (context, decisions, progress) is the durable thing; the surface is just a window onto it.

This is why module 10's context hygiene matters: you resume the rot along with the progress.

## Choosing

- **Local** for your uncommitted mess and full tooling
- **Web** for fire-and-forget tasks on committed code
- **Remote control** for "my machine, my session, but I'm not at the desk"

<Callout kind="info">
This lesson is conceptual — practice happens on your own machine and account, not in this app's sandboxed terminal.
</Callout>
