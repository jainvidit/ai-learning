Back in module 04, you learned to ask for steps written into the reply — visible reasoning you can audit. Newer models can also think privately before a single word of the answer appears: a scratchpad you don't see. That's **extended thinking**.

## The whiteboard hour analogy

Asking for a quick answer is catching a colleague in the hallway. Asking for extended thinking is booking them a quiet hour with a whiteboard. Same colleague, very different depth — and the hour costs more.

These are different tools. Visible steps (module 04) are for YOU to audit — you can point at step 3 and correct it. Extended thinking is for the MODEL — it explores, backtracks, and self-corrects before committing. You can ask for both: think deeply, then show a short reasoned summary.

## Thinking budgets

Apps and products often let you dial how much internal thinking the model may do — sometimes labeled **effort**: low, medium, high. More budget means more exploration, which means better answers on genuinely hard problems. It also means slower, costlier ones. On easy questions, extra thinking buys nothing.

You also steer effort with words: "think hard about this before answering", "take your time and consider edge cases", "double-check the arithmetic". And you can **aim** the thinking: name the part that deserves it. "The tricky part is the overlapping schedules — think carefully there."

<Callout kind="tip">
Match effort to stakes and difficulty. A restaurant pick needs hallway effort; a contract comparison deserves the whiteboard hour. Asking for deep thought on trivia just makes you wait.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="info">
You usually can't read the internal scratchpad — so keep the module 04 habit of asking for a visible summary of the reasoning when you need to check it.
</Callout>
