Time to pull back the curtain. Every playground exercise since module 02 ended with a graded checklist. Who graded you? Not a human — another AI. Meet your teacher.

## The rubric

A **rubric** is a list of **criteria**, each with a **weight**. Weights sum to 100 — it's the grading sheet a teacher fills in.

Here's a real rubric from this module (lesson 03's persona exercise). You were graded on exactly this:

| Criterion | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| expertise | The persona defines specific expertise: domain, depth, and a specialty | 25 |
| audience | The persona defines the audience it is speaking to | 25 |
| constraints | The persona sets at least two standing behavioral constraints | 30 |
| boundary | The persona includes at least one boundary where it defers or declines | 20 |

## The LLM-as-judge pattern

The app hands a second model your prompt, the rubric, and instructions to return met/not-met per criterion with feedback. A judge with a grading sheet — the same trick that grades essays, code reviews, and quality checks across the AI industry.

## Observable criteria

The key design rule: judges are only reliable when criteria are OBSERVABLE — checkable by pointing at the text. "The prompt names a specific audience" is checkable. "The prompt is good" is a vibe.

Notice every rubric in this app judges your PROMPT text, never the model's reply. The reply varies run to run; your prompt is the stable evidence.

## Weights are priorities

The core skill weighs most, and the passing score is set so you can miss the lowest-weight criterion and still pass. Deliberate design, not accident.

## Rubric-aware prompting

Flip it around: when you know the grading sheet, satisfy it explicitly, criterion by criterion. This is also how experts work with AI — state your own success criteria IN the prompt and ask the model to check its answer against them before finishing.

<Callout kind="info">
Judges aren't perfect — they're models too, and can misread. That's why good rubrics stick to observable criteria and why real systems spot-check the judge. Module 13 goes deep on writing evaluation rubrics for production.
</Callout>
