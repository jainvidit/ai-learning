## Time to drive

Below you'll find a real terminal connected to a sandbox folder containing a tiny project someone else wrote — a "recipe costs" calculator for a cafe. Your job is NOT to understand the files yourself. It's to make Claude understand them FOR you.

Here's what's in the sandbox (roughly): a note file and two small JavaScript files. JavaScript is a language of instructions for the computer. You will never need to read it unaided — that's what Claude is for.

<Callout kind="info">
Remember: the sandbox is a disposable copy. Nothing you do here can break anything. If you want a fresh start, just reset the exercise.
</Callout>

## How to drive

Type a plain-English request in the terminal below and press Enter. Or click one of the suggested prompts to start.

Watch what happens. You'll SEE Claude announce its reads before it answers: "Reading recipes.js" or "Reading README.md" — that's step 2 of the agent loop, live. It gathers context first, then answers from the actual files.

<Callout kind="tip">
Ask like you'd ask a colleague standing at the filing cabinet: "What's in this project?" or "Which recipe is most expensive to make?" Give goals, not commands. Claude figures out which files to read and what to look for.
</Callout>

## Your first exploration

Complete the three tasks below by asking Claude in plain English. If an answer seems vague, push back: "Check the file again and show me the exact line."

## What you just did

You delegated reading. Claude answered from the actual files in front of it — not from training memory (remember the cutoff lesson from module 03: context beats recall).

Notice what you didn't need to know: which files existed, what language they were in, or how to open them. You stated what you wanted to find out, and the loop handled the rest.

<Callout kind="info">
If Claude's answer ever looks off, ask it to double-check the file. Agents can misread too — but the loop makes correction cheap. Just ask again.
</Callout>
