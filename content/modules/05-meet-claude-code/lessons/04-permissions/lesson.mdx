## An AI with hands needs a leash

Think back to lessons 02 and 03. Claude read files freely, but edits and commands worked because this app pre-approved a narrow set of actions for each exercise. In real-world use, YOU hold that approval power.

An AI that can take real actions — editing files, running commands — needs a permission system. You control the leash.

## The three tiers

Here's what Claude Code does freely, what it asks about, and what it won't do:

### Reading (allowed by default)
Looking at files inside your project folder is generally allowed. Reading can't break anything — it's just looking.

### Acting (asks first)
Editing files or running commands triggers a permission prompt. Claude shows you EXACTLY what it wants to do and waits for your yes or no.

### Out of bounds (blocked)
Files outside the project, or commands that could cause harm, are heavily guarded or blocked entirely by default.

## A helpful analogy

Think of a contractor working in your house:

- Walking around and measuring? Fine — looking is harmless.
- Knocking a hole in the wall? They show you the plan and you sign off first.
- Your neighbor's house? Never, no matter how helpfully they ask.

## When Claude asks: your choices

When a permission prompt appears, you have three choices:

1. **Allow once** — just this action, this time
2. **Allow for the session** — don't ask again for this KIND of action during this session (convenience)
3. **Deny** — block it

Start cautious. As trust grows, you can loosen permissions. Real Claude Code also lets you pre-approve specific narrow actions in a config file — exactly what this app does per exercise. You'll configure that yourself in a later module.

<Callout kind="warning">
The permission prompt is the ONE moment you're the safety system. Read what it says it will do. Approving without reading is how surprises happen.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="info">
Why be so careful when the sandbox is disposable? Because habits transfer. Practice the read-then-approve reflex here, where mistakes are free, and you'll have it when it matters.
</Callout>

## What if you don't recognize a command?

Claude Code asks to run something you don't understand? Ask back: "Explain in plain language what that command does and why you need it."

Claude will answer. Then you decide. You're allowed to interrogate before approving — that's the whole point of the prompt.
