Here's the trap: humans use confidence as a shortcut for competence.

A hesitant friend gets fact-checked. A fluent, confident one doesn't. We assume that certainty signals expertise.

LLMs break this heuristic completely.

## Why Confident Tone Means Nothing

Remember what the model does: it predicts the most likely next word, trained on mountains of internet text.

That training text — articles, textbooks, explanations, forum answers — overwhelmingly *sounds sure of itself*. Confident phrasing is the most likely continuation style. It's produced the same way whether the underlying fact is right or invented.

Tone is part of the prediction, not a report on internal certainty.

## The Fluent Narrator

Think of the model as a **fluent narrator**, not a sworn witness.

Both its right answers and its wrong answers come out in the same polished voice. The sonnet about the French Revolution and the completely invented statistic? Same confident delivery.

## So How Do You Operate?

You need a stakes-based rule:

- **Low stakes** (brainstorms, drafts, creative ideas): Just use it. If a name for your book club is wrong, who cares? Usefulness is the only test.
- **Medium stakes** (facts you'll repeat, plans you'll follow): Verify the load-bearing facts against a real source before you act.
- **High stakes** (medical, legal, financial, safety): AI is an assistant to a professional or primary source, never a substitute. A medication dosage? Talk to a doctor. A filing deadline? Check the government website.

## Verification Moves That Work

When verification matters, here's what actually helps:

1. **Ask for sources, then CHECK one.** Don't just accept the citation — open it and confirm it says what the model claims.
2. **Ask the same question in a fresh conversation.** Remember randomness from Module 01? If you get confident but *contradictory* answers, that's a red flag: the model is sampling plausible text, not retrieving a solid fact.
3. **Paste in an authoritative document** and ask again, grounded in it. If the model's answer from memory contradicts the document you just gave it, trust the document.

<Callout kind="warning">
"It sounded really sure" is never a reason to skip checking. Certainty of tone is the cheapest thing a language model produces.
</Callout>

## The Module Wrap

You've now mapped four edges:

1. The **attention budget** — the context window is finite, and what you put where matters.
2. The **knowledge cutoff** — if the answer depends on "now" and you didn't provide "now", it's a guess.
3. **Reasoning vs recall** — construction tasks are shakier than retrieval tasks.
4. **Confident wrongness** — fluency is not evidence.

Knowing these edges is what lets you use the tool *boldly* everywhere else. You're not walking on eggshells, afraid everything is wrong. You're calibrated: you know when to trust, when to verify, and when to reach for a different tool.

That's the foundation for real, confident AI use.
