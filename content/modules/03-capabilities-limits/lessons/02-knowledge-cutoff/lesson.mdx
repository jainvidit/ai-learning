Imagine a very well-read person who has been in a windowless room since a certain date. They're encyclopedic about everything *before* that date, but everything after? They're just guessing.

That's what a **knowledge cutoff** is.

## Why It Exists

Remember from Module 01: the model learned during training, from a huge snapshot of internet text collected up to a certain point in time. That cutoff date is the model's **knowledge cutoff**.

Training is enormously expensive — millions of dollars and months of compute time. Models aren't continuously retrained every day with fresh news. The snapshot freezes, and the model's learned knowledge freezes with it.

## The Danger Zone

Here's the tricky part: the model often won't say "I don't know that yet."

Asked about events after its cutoff — last night's game, this quarter's phone prices, who won yesterday's election — it may hallucinate plausible-sounding answers. Remember hallucination from Module 01? Now you know one of its biggest triggers: prediction tries to fill the gap with likely-looking text, and there's nothing in training to stop it from making things up.

## What's Affected, What's Not

Questions that depend on "now" are risky:
- News, current events, sports results
- "Latest" versions of products, apps, or frameworks
- Current prices, office-holders, top-ten lists

Stable knowledge is mostly safe:
- History (as long as it's not *recent* history)
- Science basics, cooking, language, math concepts
- How-to knowledge that doesn't change often

## The Workaround That Matters

The context window from Lesson 01 is your door into the windowless room.

Paste in the current article. Include the latest price list. Tell the model today's date. The model uses information in its context even when training never saw it.

Some AI products do this automatically — they fetch a web search result and paste it into the context for you. That's the same trick, just automated.

<Callout kind="warning">
If the answer depends on "now" and you didn't provide "now", treat the answer as a guess.
</Callout>

## Looking Forward

Telling the model what it can't know is half of prompting well. You'll see this principle again and again as you learn to work with AI: the more context *you* supply, the less the model has to guess.
