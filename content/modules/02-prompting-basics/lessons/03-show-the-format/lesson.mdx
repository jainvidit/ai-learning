Imagine ordering a cake and saying "something nice." You might get a beautiful three-tier wedding cake when you needed cupcakes for six people. Or the other way around.

Formats are like showing the baker a photo. The model happily produces essays when you wanted a table — unless you say so.

## Three levers: length, structure, style

You control the shape of the answer with three things:

**Length:** "under 100 words," "exactly 5 bullets," "one paragraph"

**Structure:** "a table with columns X/Y/Z," "numbered steps," "subject line + 3 short paragraphs"

**Style:** "plain language, no jargon," "formal," "like a friendly text message"

Here's the same request with and without format instructions:

**No format:** "Help me pack for a trip."

**With format:** "Give me a packing checklist grouped under Clothes / Toiletries / Documents, max 5 items per group."

The first one might get you a friendly essay. The second one gets you a usable list.

## The strongest move: show an example

The model is a pattern continuer — you learned that in module 01. So give it a pattern to continue.

Instead of describing the format in words, show a tiny example:

"Format each item like: `Name — one-line why it matters`"

Now the model knows exactly what shape to produce. One demonstration often beats a whole paragraph of description.

<Callout kind="tip">
If you'll reuse the output somewhere — a spreadsheet, an email, a slide — describe THAT destination. "Formatted so I can paste it into a spreadsheet" does a lot of work in one phrase.
</Callout>

## Before and after

Same task, different format instructions:

**Loose:** "What should I pack for a weekend trip to the mountains?"

**Shaped:** "What should I pack for a weekend trip to the mountains in October? Give me a bulleted list under three headings: Clothes, Gear, and Essentials. Keep each section to 4–5 items, one line each."

The second version tells the model the structure (bulleted list, three headings), the length (4–5 items per section), and the style (one line each). You get a clean, scannable list instead of a rambling paragraph.
