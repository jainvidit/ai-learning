Here's the reframe that separates beginners from pros:

**The first answer is a draft, not a verdict.**

Professionals don't write perfect prompts on the first try. They iterate fast. Getting a so-so first answer isn't failure — it's the normal first step.

Think of it like a haircut. You don't walk out at the first snip. You say "shorter on the sides," they adjust, you look again. Same thing here.

## Bad iteration: hoping for better luck

This doesn't work:

**You:** "Write a summary of this article."  
**Model:** [generic paragraph]  
**You:** "No, that's wrong, try again."  
**Model:** [different generic paragraph]

What happened? You gave the model no new information, so it just re-rolled the dice. Remember from module 01: the model has randomness built in, so reruns vary. But they don't vary *intelligently* unless you steer.

"Try again" tells the model nothing. You get a different flavor of the same miss.

## Good iteration: targeted feedback

Here's what works:

Name what to **KEEP**, what to **CHANGE**, and (ideally) **WHY**.

**You:** "Write a summary of this article."  
**Model:** [generic paragraph]  
**You:** "Keep the friendly tone. The second paragraph is too apologetic — make it more confident, and cut the last line entirely."  
**Model:** [better version]

Now the model knows what you liked and what to fix. It's working with you, not guessing.

## The conversation is your context

Everything you've said so far stays in front of the model. That means your follow-up can be short — it builds on what's already there.

You don't need to repeat the whole request. "Make the middle section shorter" is a complete instruction when the model already knows what the middle section is.

The whole conversation is the model's working memory for this task.

## When to start over instead

If you've corrected the model three or four times and it keeps drifting back to the same mistakes, the conversation is full of wrong turns. Those wrong turns keep steering the predictions.

Best move: **start fresh.** Write a new prompt that includes everything you learned to specify.

<Callout kind="tip">
Your failed conversation is research. It taught you exactly what to spell out up front next time. That's not wasted effort — that's the fastest way to learn what good prompts for YOUR task look like.
</Callout>

A clean slate with an upgraded prompt usually beats a fifth correction.

## Your turn

Practice giving targeted feedback. You'll see a bad first draft and your job is to steer it to something better — not by saying "try again," but by naming exactly what to keep and what to fix.

## You've got the four habits

Here's what you now know how to do:

1. **Be specific** — state the task, audience, and constraints
2. **Give context and a role** — supply the background facts and perspective the answer needs
3. **Show the format** — control length, structure, and style
4. **Iterate** — treat the first answer as a draft and give targeted feedback

These four habits will take you a long way. As you keep practicing, they'll become automatic — you'll write better first prompts and steer faster when you need to.

In a future module, you'll learn the pro techniques that build on these foundations. For now, you've got everything you need to have much better AI conversations.
